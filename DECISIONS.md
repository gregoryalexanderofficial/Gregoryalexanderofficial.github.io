## 2026-11-10 — Architecture (decided by Greg, before the first build)

- **Canonical copy:** The site's files live in ONE place: a GitHub repository
  owned by Gregory Alexander's own GitHub account. This `site/` folder is the
  starter copy that becomes the repository's contents. Never maintain a
  second, divergent copy — if it isn't in the repo, it isn't the site.
- **Hosting:** GitHub Pages publishes the repo automatically. Saving a change
  to the files republishes the site at the same public link, usually within
  about a minute. Publishing is switched on once, in the repo's Settings.
- **Who can edit:** Greg (owner) and Avril Johnnidis (collaborator). Each
  works through their own AI tools connected to the repository, or through
  the GitHub web interface. Access is granted deliberately by invite; the
  public link is for viewing only.
- **Turn-taking norm:** Editing is sequential, not simultaneous. Whoever
  saves changes tells the other person, so nobody builds on a stale copy.
  The repo's history shows what changed, line by line.
- **Content rule:** Site copy comes from Greg's approved materials only.
  Don't invent claims, credits, or bio language. Placeholders marked
  `[to be written]` are deliberate gaps awaiting Greg's words.

## Design intentions (Greg, Tue Nov 10, 2026)
- The starter page in this folder is SCAFFOLDING ONLY: proof the machinery works (one link, one canonical copy), not a design candidate. Its look must not anchor anyone. The real design gets dreamed first.
- Design dreaming/scheming is a planned use of the first Greg + Avril call: the ideal look and feel, dreamed together, before any serious building.
- Exemplar Greg admires: Audrey's site. Less about her as a figure, more about what she does. A dynamic water droplet falling into a lake; clicking it opens the page: clean links over a beautiful high-def watery background, links to her recorded tracks and information about them. Alive and moving, not static.
- Greg's own floated image: land on his smiling face, click his sparkling eye, and it takes you in. (Same move as the droplet: one evocative image as the door.)
- Principles: the design may be complex in construction but must OCCUR simple. All of Greg's domains/hats present, but encountered simply and accessibly. A visitor should easily find ways to work with him and reasons to work with him. NO overwhelm: not piles of menus, lists, words, copy, descriptions, or titles with parameters and rates.
