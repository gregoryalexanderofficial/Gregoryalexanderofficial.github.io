## Part 1: The project, in brief

**The site is live:** https://gregoryalexanderofficial.github.io/
**The shared workshop (the files):** https://github.com/gregoryalexanderofficial/Gregoryalexanderofficial.github.io

How it works:

- The site's files live in one place, a GitHub repository under Greg's own account. One copy, always the current one. No emailing versions back and forth.
- Saving a change republishes the site at the same public link, usually within about a minute, so both people can look at the same thing while talking.
- Greg is the owner. Avril joins as a collaborator by invite. The invite needs her GitHub username; that is the one blocking item.
- Each person edits through their own AI tools connected to the repository, or directly in GitHub's web pages. Editing is sequential, not simultaneous: whoever saves changes tells the other.
- Decisions get written down in the project's DECISIONS.md file, so nothing evaporates between calls.

What is already in the workshop:

- The starter site itself: one page (hero, Listen, About, Offerings, Contact) built only from approved copy. It is scaffolding, proof the machinery works, NOT a design candidate. Its look should not anchor anyone.
- `_undecided/`: candidate materials being weighed, including this document. Underscore folders are not published as part of the site.
- `_old-website/`: the salvage from Greg's old Wix site, complete. All 40 media files, the Home and Bio page copy verbatim, and the site's links. The Wix account turned out to hold no custom domain and no billing or subscriptions, so nothing is being paid for and nothing is lost by leaving it.

### Rates reference and philosophy (internal reference, NOT website copy)

Greg's direction (November 12, 2026): the official rate information lives in this
section as reference, in case it is ever needed. None of it is approved website copy.
Whether rates are mentioned anywhere on the website at all is undecided, and his
stated site principle leans against menus, lists, and rates on the site. What follows
is in two parts: the numbers, then the reasoning and philosophy around them, which
Greg wants held in the project's working materials even where it would never be
published as copy.

The numbers:

Teaching studio (private sessions, his home studio in Windsor Park or the student's home):

- 30 minutes: $40
- 45 minutes: $50
- 60 minutes: $60
- Four 60-minute sessions: $200
- Public rate line: "Starting at $60/hour. Project and event pricing available upon request."
- When someone asks his lesson rate, he simply says $60 an hour.

Performances, sessions, and project work (accompanying, recording sessions, music
direction, ceremony and event work):

- Priced by scope. There is no single published rate; inquiries are handled case by case: contact for specific rates.
- The professional range for this kind of work runs up to $200 to $300 an hour depending on the project.

The philosophy (Greg's reasoning, November 12, 2026; internal, never copy):

- There is a sliding scale aesthetic underneath all of this, even though he does not advertise a sliding scale.
- The $60 lesson rate already feels like the lower, entry-level end to him, which is why he states it plainly and does not volunteer discounts.
- Flexibility runs in both directions. If someone genuinely wants to study with him and can only afford something like $40 an hour, he would probably still consider it, because the wanting matters. Nobody is currently asking.
- At the other end, with someone well off who really wants to work with him, he would almost prefer to charge more than the standard rate. That clientele is not banging down the door yet; the orientation stands anyway.
- His working sense for project work is that the right project at around $100 an hour would feel good. That is reasoning, not language: he has said explicitly it should not appear as copy.

Draft copy candidates, kept minimal (DRAFT, awaiting Greg's approval):

- For the project range, no numbers: "For performances, sessions, and projects, pricing depends on the scope of the work. I am glad to talk and find the number that fits."
- A sliding scale signal, if one is ever wanted in copy: "If the standard rate is the barrier, say so. The right fit matters more."

## Part 3: Design intentions (Greg's, November 10, 2026)

- The design gets dreamed first, together, before any serious building.
- Exemplar Greg admires: Audrey's site. It is less about her as a figure and more about what she does. A dynamic water droplet falls into a lake; clicking it opens the page: clean links over a beautiful, moving, watery background, her tracks one click away. Alive, not static.
- Greg's own floated image for his door: land on his smiling face, click his sparkling eye, and it takes you in. Same move as the droplet: one evocative image as the threshold.
- Principles: the design may be complex in construction but must occur simple in experience. All of Greg's domains and hats are present, but encountered simply and accessibly. A visitor should easily find ways to work with him, and reasons to work with him. No overwhelm: no piles of menus, lists, descriptions, or rates.
